Compressor Proving Troubleshooting

Are you looking for this Symbio 700 Compressor Proving Circuit Troubleshooting for Precedent and Large Foundation? For units with ReliaTel controls (RTRM), see the ReliaTel Compressor Proving article, and for units with a Symbio 700 unit controller for Odyssey (TTA or TWA), see the Odyssey Compressor Proving article.

This troubleshooting guide applies to Symbio 700 unit controllers on Precedent and Large Foundation units with model number prefixes: Precedent - DHJ, DHK, DSJ, DSK, THJ, THK, TSJ, TSK, TZK, WHJ, WHK, WSJ, WSK, YHJ, YHK, YSJ, YSK, and YZK, and Large Foundation - EDK and GDK. Use it when diagnosing compressor 1 proving, compressor 2 proving, compressor proving trip, compressor proving lockout, and compressor contactor fail alarms. It covers compressor will not start, contactor not pulling in, proving circuit open, relay contact resistance, auxiliary switch checks, and the exact connector and wire paths used to verify the circuit.

Symptoms and alarm codes

Use this section to match the alarm behavior to the likely fault.

  • Alarm - Compressor Proving Trip Diagnostic – 15-minute lockout: The HPC or DTL opens while the compressor is running after the circuit has proved, and the Compressor Contactor loses its 24 VAC, causing the Aux Switch to open.
  • Alarm - Compressor Proving Lockout Diagnostic – INSTANT UNIT LOCKOUT!: The Symbio Board calls for the Compressor Contactor, and the Contactor doesn't pull in within 7 seconds.
  • Alarm - Compressor Proving Lockout Diagnostic – INSTANT UNIT LOCKOUT!: The Symbio Board calls for the Compressor Contactor; it pulls in and drops back out after 7 seconds.
  • Alarm - Compressor Contactor Fail Diagnostic – INSTANT UNIT LOCKOUT!: The Compressor Contactor Aux Switch closes before it is supposed to.

Possible causes: HPC and/or DTL open, contactor coil open, wiring/connection error, no 24 VAC at the black wire on the Aux Switch, bad Aux Switch, Aux Switch not on the contactor correctly, technician manually pushes in the contactor, or the contactor is welded shut.

An Important verification that is often overlooked

If the HPC or DTL opened while the compressor was already running and the circuit had previously proved, do notassume the fault is only in the proving wiring, voltage, resistance, or board issue. This type of trip can also indicate a refrigerant-system problem, such as incorrect charge, restricted airflow, condenser issues, or another condition that causes abnormal head pressure or temperature. Power down the unit and unplug the compressor. Isolate power, unplug the compressor, and wait about 15 minutes before determining the next step.

If the fault does not return quickly while the compressor is unplugged, focus first on troubleshooting the refrigerant circuit rather than assuming a wiring, voltage, resistance, or board issue. If you find no refrigerant-side issue, continue below to test the Compressor Proving wiring.

Compressor 1

Compressor 1: 24VAC Voltage Tests

Verify if 24VAC control power is present at the Unit Controller.

  • Setup: Unit powered ON. Set your multimeter to VAC.
  • Reference Point: Place your black meter lead on clean Chassis Ground.

Voltage Diagnostics Table

 

UC-P8-4 Power Supply Path

If UC-P8-4 has no voltage, track back through these connectors in order to find where the power is lost:

  1. AB-J20-4 (🟠 Orange wire)
  2. AB-J8-1 (🔵 Blue wire)
  3. PPM72 / PPF72-3 (🔵 Blue wire)
  4. PPF2 / PPM2-1 (🔵 Blue wire)
  5. TNS2 (Transformer 2)
Compressor 1: Resistance & Safety Circuit Tests

If you have 24VAC leaving the controller on UC-P8-5 but the contactor coil (CC1) refuses to pull in, check the safety limit switches and the contactor coil resistance.

  • Setup: Unit powered OFF. Set your multimeter to Resistance (Ω).

 

Resistance Diagnostics Table

 

Compressor 1 Run Circuit Paths

Revision C (Older Boards)

If you measure an open circuit (O.L.), trace these connection points in order with your meter leads to locate the break:

  • UC-P8-5 (🟡 Yellow) to AB-J13-4 (🟠 Orange)
  • DTL1 (🔴 Red wires) (Discharge Temperature Limit)
  • AB-J13-3 (🔵 Blue) to AB-J13-2 (🔴 Red)
  • HCP1 (🔴 Red wires) (High Pressure Control)
  • AB-J13-1 (⚫ Black) to AB-J20-5 (🟡 Yellow)

 

Revision E (Newer Boards with Test Pins)

If your board has physical test pins, trace along this specific path:

Path: J20-4 > UC-J8-4 > UC-J8-5 > J20-5 > Test Pin Comp 1 Run > J13-1 > BK HPC1 > R HPC1 > J13-2 > Test Pin HPC1 > J13-3 > BL DTL1 > O DTL1 > J13-4 > Test Pin DLT1 > J5-9 > O/BK CC1 > Y/BK CC1 > J5-10

 

CC1 Coil Backtrace Path

If your coil resistance test is O.L., trace through these physical plugs:

  1. PPF81 / PPM81 (🟠/⚫ Pin 9 or 🟡/⚫ Pin 10)
  2. PPF23 (🟠/⚫ Pin 1) or PPM23 (⚫ Pin 1)
  3. CC1 Contactor Coil Terminals (⚫ Black wires)
Compressor 1: Auxiliary Contact Feedback Tests

These tests confirm that the contactor auxiliary switch successfully tells the Unit Controller the contactor has pulled in.

Revision C

 

Aux Voltage & Feedback Tests (Unit Powered ON)

  1. Check Input Power:
    • Measure VAC from UC-P8-2 (🔴 Red) to Chassis Ground.
    • Expected: 24VAC.
    • If no power: Check AB-J18-1 (sourced from UC-P3-1). If UC-P3-1 is dead, trace back: UC-J4-1 > PPF72/PPM72 > PPF1/PPM1 > TNS1 (Transformer 1).
  2. Check Proving Output:
    • When the CC1 contactor closes, the auxiliary contacts must close and send 24VAC back to UC-P8-3 (🔵 Blue).

Aux Resistance Test (Unit Powered OFF)

  1. Set meter to Resistance (Ω).
  2. Place one lead on UC-P8-3 (🔵 Blue) and the other lead on UC-P8-2 (🔴 Red).
  3. Manually push in the CC1 contactor plunger.
  4. Expected: ≤ 0.6 Ω. If higher or open, the auxiliary block contacts are dirty/failed and must be replaced.

 

Compressor 1 Auxiliary Contact Wiring Paths

Revision C Path

  • Yellow Wire Leg: CC1 Aux N.O. (🟡 Y) > Plug PPF81/PPM81-5 (🟡 Y) > AB-J5-5 (🟡 Y) > AB-J20-2 (🔴 R) > UC-P8-2 (🔴 R)
  • Brown Wire Leg: CC1 Aux N.O. (🟤 BR) > Plug PPF81/PPM81-6 (🟤 BR) > AB-J5-6 (🟤 BR) > AB-J20-3 (🔵 BL) > UC-P8-3 (🔵 BL)

Board Diagnostic: Relay Contact Resistance Check

If you suspect the Symbio 700 board's internal compressor relays are damaged (pitted or sticking), perform this manual test using your phone's service app.

Compressor 1 (CC1 Relay) Check Steps
  1. Unplug the UC-J8 harness from the Red P8 connector on the Symbio board.
    • Take note: Orange wire is UC-P8-4, Yellow wire is UC-P8-5.
  2. Connect to the board with the Symbio Mobile App. Go to Tools > Service Test Mode.
  3. Force initiate Cool 2 in the app to energize the relay. LED 5 on the board must turn ON.
  4. Measure Resistance (Ω) directly across board pins P8-4 and P8-5.
    • You only have a 5-second window to read this.
  5. Specification: Must be < 10 Ω. Repeat this test 3–4 times. If it ever reads above 10 ohms, replace the board.

Compressor 2 (CC2 Relay) Check Steps
  1. Unplug the UC-J9 harness from the White UC-P9 connector on the Symbio board.
    • Take note: Yellow/Black wire is UC-P9-4, Brown/Black wire is UC-P9-5.
  2. Connect to the board with the Symbio Mobile App. Go to Tools > Service Test Mode.
  3. Force initiate Cool 1 in the app to energize the relay. LED 15 on the board must turn ON.
  4. Measure Resistance (Ω) directly across board pins P9-4 and P9-5.
    • You only have a 5-second window to read this.
  5. Specification: Must be < 10 Ω. Repeat this test 3–4 times. If it ever reads above 10 ohms, replace the board.
